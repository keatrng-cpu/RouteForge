What it does

The planner, minus the parts everyone hates.

Each feature below exists because a market leader gets it wrong. We read the reviews so you don't have to live in them.

Discovery

Stops worth the detour

Tell it what you actually like — weird roadside stuff, good coffee, short hikes — and get stops along your real driving corridor, not a generic top-ten list.

Freshness

Live-checked, not shelf-aged

Suggestions are verified against the live web at generation time. No decade-old database quietly recommending a restaurant that closed in 2019.

Honesty

Confidence on every stop

A three-dot gauge shows how sure the model is that a place exists as described — plus a verify link straight to the map. Trust, but check.

Day logic

Days that split themselves

Set one slider — max hours behind the wheel — and the trip breaks into days automatically. All of the power planners' logic, none of their config screens.

Navigation

One tap into Google Maps

We do discovery and day-splitting; your nav app does the turns. Each day exports as a ready-made route, chunked to never hit a waypoint cap.

Big rigs

RV mode, honestly labeled

Biases every suggestion toward big-vehicle parking and access, and flags a clearance advisory on each stop. An advisory — we'll tell you plainly when it isn't certified routing.

Collaboration

Share codes, free for everyone

Send a code, they open the trip. Collaborators never hit a paywall for features you paid for — the whole point of planning together.

Control

Your stops outrank ours

Add, reorder, or delete anything. The AI proposes; you decide. Your hand-added stops are marked as yours and never overwritten.

Memory

Trips that survive the drive

Save a route and it's still there next season — with the notes, the order, and the stops you actually loved. Plans shouldn't evaporate at the destination.

The rule

AI that never makes up your numbers.

Every drive time, detour, and day total is either computed, retrieved, or shown as a question mark. Our model can explain a route, order it, and argue for a stop — but it is structurally forbidden from inventing a single figure. When it doesn't know, it says so. That's the rule, and it's the only reason to trust a planner at all.
